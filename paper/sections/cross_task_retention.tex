\subsection{Retention across all six methods}\label{app:retention-patterns}

We compare LoRA's forgetting with \emph{each of the other five methods} across twelve settings, comprising six mathematics model/capacity pairs, two coding backbones and four cat budgets. Each method's LR is chosen by task performance on the development set. We compare forgetting within each setting, so language NLL and image drift remain on their own scales.

\paragraph{LoRA most consistently limits forgetting.}
LoRA has the lowest mean forgetting in \result{rob_lora_first_original}/12 settings. OFT, MiLoRA and HRA each have the lowest forgetting in one setting, and DoRA and PiSSA in none (\Cref{tab:retention-all-methods}). LoRA also has the lowest average rank, \result{rob_original_rank}, followed by DoRA at 2.75. The three exceptions are small-capacity Qwen, where LoRA's outlying high-LR selection forgets most, large-capacity Llama, where OFT has the lowest forgetting, and cat rank 8, where HRA has the lowest forgetting.

\paragraph{Consistency across selection and weighting choices.}
First, we choose LR using two seeds and measure forgetting on the remaining seed, repeating for every seed. The original grids, eligibility rules and smaller-LR tie rule remain unchanged. LoRA still has the lowest mean forgetting in \result{rob_lora_first_other_seeds}/12 settings. Each other method does so in at most one. Its average rank remains lowest (\result{rob_other_seeds_rank}, versus 2.50 for DoRA).
Second, we give each model/task group equal importance, preventing groups with more capacities from dominating the summary.
Third, we remove each backbone or individual setting in turn.
Finally, we compare results using one language seed and one image seed at a time, covering all nine combinations rather than matching unrelated seed numbers.

Across all these checks, LoRA has the lowest forgetting in more settings than any other method (\Cref{tab:retention-robustness}). This supports the claim that LoRA most consistently limits forgetting across the tested methods. After LR selection on other seeds, DoRA has a better average rank in three seed combinations and when FLUX is removed.

\paragraph{Task scores and PiSSA's retention costs.}
DoRA has higher mean task scores than LoRA in 10/14 comparisons, covering two of six mathematics settings, all four coding comparisons and all four cat budgets. This supports the reported trend in mean task scores. Its coding and cat gains accompany additional forgetting.

With backbone, capacity and LR fixed across all six methods, PiSSA forgets most in all \result{cross_math_matched_pissa_highest}/\result{cross_math_matched_blocks} complete mathematics comparisons and \result{cross_coding_matched_pissa_highest}/\result{cross_coding_matched_blocks} coding comparisons, including all \result{cross_math_matched_pissa_seed_highest} and \result{cross_coding_matched_pissa_seed_highest} seed comparisons. In cat, it ranks highest in \result{cross_cat_matched_pissa_highest}/\result{cross_cat_matched_blocks} comparisons and among the highest two in all \result{cross_cat_matched_pissa_highest_two}. HRA ranks lowest in \result{cross_cat_hra_matched_lowest}/\result{cross_cat_matched_blocks}. These matched-LR comparisons reinforce the finding that PiSSA often incurs greater retention costs and show that HRA can retain well in specific settings.

\paragraph{LoRA versus OFT across objects.}
After averaging seeds within each object, LoRA has lower drift than OFT for 23, 27, 28 and 28 of the 28 objects across increasing budgets. This extends LoRA's image-drift advantage beyond cat personalization.
